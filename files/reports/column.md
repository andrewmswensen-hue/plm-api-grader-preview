## Context
- Software category: banking or payments
- What the API is for and its core objects and workflows: The Column API is the programmatic interface to a nationally chartered US bank. It exists so a developer can open and manage FDIC-insured bank accounts, move money over ACH, Fedwire, book (internal), RTP/FedNow realtime and check rails, and read back balances, transactions and settlement data. Its core objects are entities (account owners), bank accounts and account numbers, counterparties, transfers of each rail type, events, and settlement reports. Its core workflows are opening accounts under a verified entity, initiating and managing the lifecycle of payments, and reconciling balances and transactions.

## Category 2: API Design, Reliability, and Operability: 7.5/10
- **C2.1 Modern API conventions: yes** - Resource-oriented REST over HTTPS with JSON and standard verbs, described by a published OpenAPI 3.1.0 specification (82 GET, 72 POST, 14 PATCH, 8 DELETE, 1 PUT). Verified live across GET, POST, PATCH and DELETE. [https://raw.githubusercontent.com/column/openapi/main/openapi.yaml]
- **C2.2 Consistent typing: partial** - Core live reads are type-clean: on `GET /bank-accounts/{id}` all four balance fields are integers (cents), `is_interest_bearing` and `is_overdraftable` are real booleans, and identifiers are strings. The exact limitation: monetary values are not consistently typed across the API. `GET /bank-accounts/{id}/history` documents every balance figure as a *string* (`"available_balance_credit": "100"`, `"available_balance_debit": "-200"`) while the same concepts are integers on the bank account object and `int64` in settlement reports; and the loan sale object mixes types within a single response (`"amount": 100900` as an integer alongside `"sold_principal_receivable": "100000"` and `"paid_servicing_fee": "100"` as strings). Two further inconsistencies were observed live: the same `created_at` field name is returned at three different precisions in one webhook payload (`2026-09-02T18:50:54Z`, `2026-09-02T18:50:53.98882748Z`, and `2026-09-02T18:50:39.719Z` on the endpoint object), and transfer `status` is returned uppercase live (`COMPLETED`, `HOLD`, `CANCELED`) while the documented example response shows lowercase (`"status": "completed"`). These are confined to secondary/reporting endpoints and to value formatting rather than the core balance and transfer reads. [https://docs.column.com/api/bank-account/get-bank-account-summary-history; https://docs.column.com/api/lending; live observations 2026-09-02]
- **C2.3 Structured errors: partial** - The error *body* is excellent and among the best available: a stable machine-readable `code`, a categorical `type`, a developer `message`, a `documentation_url`, and a `details` object naming the exact offending field, with all codes and types enumerated in the docs. Verified live: `{"code":"mandatory_parameter_missing","details":{"field":"entity_id"},"type":"validation_error"}`. The exact limitation is HTTP status semantics, which the check requires for a yes. Column's own documentation states `404` means "Not Found. The requested resource does not exist", but not-found conditions return **HTTP 400** in practice, confirmed on three separate resources (`bank_account_not_found`, and `resource_not_found` on both `/transfers/ach/{id}` and `/counterparties/{id}`). Additionally a `401` returns an entirely empty body (`content-length: 0`) with no structured error, despite `authentication_error` being a documented error type. A third observation: appending stray trailing characters to an entity ID is silently truncated and returns HTTP 200 with the prefix-matching record rather than rejecting the malformed input, so a string-concatenation bug in operator code fails silently instead of erroring. [https://docs.column.com/working-with-the-api/errors; live observations 2026-09-02]

What this means for you: The parts that stop you losing money are genuinely strong. ## Bottom line for a property manager
Column is a real, nationally chartered bank with one of the best banking APIs you can build on, and unlike almost every "banking API" you will be pitched, there is no sponsor bank in the middle: Column holds the money itself. Today you could build automated owner disbursements, vendor payments by ACH or same-day wire, rent collection by ACH debit, check issuance and deposit, and a reconciliation feed that pulls every transaction with running balances into your own system, and you could hand an AI agent a key that reads balances but is structurally incapable of moving a dollar. What you cannot do is treat this as a substitute for your PMS or your trust accounting: the API has no concept of a property, unit, lease or tenant, and despite a polished property-management page promising trust reconciliation and owner disbursements, there is not a single property-management-specific endpoint in the API itself, so you or your software supply all of that logic on top of generic bank accounts and transfers. Be careful with two words in Column's marketing: the "trust accounts" in its 2026 changelog are legal estate-planning trusts, not client trust accounting, and the PM-specific promises (AppFolio, Yardi and Entrata sync, positive pay, trust guardrails on check intake) are product and relationship features reached through a sales conversation, not documented API capabilities. The biggest API strength is control and safety, with genuinely excellent scoped keys, proven idempotency, and signed and retried webhooks; the biggest limitation is operational opacity, with no published rate limits or retry guidance, no protection against concurrent edits, and an error-status contract that contradicts its own documentation.

On cost and access, two clarifications matter. Column has confirmed in writing that the API itself is free to use in both sandbox and production, and that the KYC/KYB onboarding required before it can open you a live bank account carries no fee. That onboarding is a legal obligation for any chartered bank under its Customer Identification Program, not a commercial hurdle, and it is not counted against Column anywhere in this score. Separately, if you want Column to originate the ACH debits that pull rent, it requires a funded, locked program reserve. Column's published going-live guide states that reserve must exceed your rolling 60-day ACH debit volume, but two other pages in the same documentation describe a Column-determined percentage that can be reduced after a financials review, and Column has confirmed the latter is correct: the reserve is set from your volume, your historical return rates and industry dynamics. Treat the going-live guide's figure as unreliable and get your number from Column directly. In practice this may not bind at all, since most property managers collect rent through their PMS rather than through their bank, and the reserve only applies if you originate those debits at Column yourself. For a several-hundred-unit operator, Column is the bank and the rails underneath your stack, and you would still need a PMS and a trust-accounting system sitting on top of it.
