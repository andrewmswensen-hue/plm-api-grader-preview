What this means for you.

One paragraph per category, in plain language.

1 · Functional Coverage

15 / 15 points

The strongest part of the API. Everything a business runs on financially is reachable in code, and you can act on it rather than just look at it: open accounts, move money five different ways, put a transfer on hold and then release or kill it, claw back an ACH, stop a check, and be told about all of it automatically. In the sandbox the grader opened an account, updated it, sent money, held and released a payment, held and cancelled another, and closed the account, all through the API.

2 · Design & Reliability

7.5 / 10 points

The parts that stop you losing money are genuinely strong. Retrying a payment cannot double-send it, proven live. Webhooks are signed and retried for three days, you can page through and bulk-export everything, and errors name the exact field you got wrong. The weak spots are the ones you hit at 2am: Column will not tell you its rate limits or when to retry, not-found comes back as the wrong HTTP code so naive error handling misclassifies it, there is no protection against two processes overwriting each other, and the request id you would hand support is real but undocumented.

3 · Access Control

5 / 5 points

A perfect score, and the category that matters most if you ever point an AI agent at a bank account. You can mint a key that reads balances and nothing else, or one that pays vendors by ACH but is structurally incapable of sending a wire, scoped to a single account. You can require a human to approve transfers from a given key, issue one key per tool, and kill any of them instantly. With a sandbox that behaves like production, you can build something risky without it ever touching real money.

4 · Docs & AI-Ready

3.8 / 5 points

If you hand Claude or ChatGPT the job of writing your Column integration, it has unusually good material: the entire documentation as one clean file built for AI tools, plus a full machine-readable spec. The catch is accuracy rather than coverage, and it is why this is not a higher mark. A webhook guide prints a URL that returns 404, three response examples show a status value in the wrong case, the error table promises a 404 the API never sends, and one endpoint listed as supporting idempotency is not in the spec at all. Build from the API reference pages rather than the narrative guides, and verify against the sandbox rather than trusting an example.

5 · Access & Cost

11.3 / 15 points

The sandbox is free, self-serve and a complete copy of the bank, which is unusually generous, and Column has confirmed in writing that the API itself is free in both sandbox and production. Two things hold this short of full marks. Production is not self-serve, and several capabilities, including positive pay and interest-bearing accounts, need Column to enable them for your platform. And if you want Column to originate the ACH debits that pull rent, it requires a funded, locked reserve. Column's own going-live guide says that reserve must exceed your rolling 60-day debit volume, while two other pages in the same documentation describe a Column-determined percentage; Column confirms the latter is correct, so get your number from them rather than the guide.

What works

  • Scoped keys down to a single rail on a single account, plus human approval
  • Idempotency proven live: the same payment sent twice created one transfer
  • Signed webhooks retried for three days, across 190 event types
  • A free sandbox that is a complete copy of the bank, every route included
  • The whole documentation published as one clean file built for AI tools

What to watch

  • No published rate limits, no Retry-After, and no backoff guidance
  • Not-found returns HTTP 400, contradicting Column's own documentation
  • No protection against two processes overwriting the same record
  • Documentation drift: a 404 URL, wrong-case examples, a phantom endpoint
  • Direct ACH debit origination needs a reserve balance, sized case by case

Category 2 · Design & Reliability

7.5 / 10
C2.1

Modern API conventions

Resource-oriented REST over HTTPS with JSON and standard verbs, described by a published OpenAPI 3.1.0 specification (82 GET, 72 POST, 14 PATCH, 8 DELETE, 1 PUT). Verified live across GET, POST, PATCH and DELETE. [https://raw.githubusercontent.com/column/openapi/main/openapi.yaml]

Yes
C2.2

Consistent typing

Core live reads are type-clean: on GET /bank-accounts/{id} all four balance fields are integers (cents), is_interest_bearing and is_overdraftable are real booleans, and identifiers are strings. The exact limitation: monetary values are not consistently typed across the API. GET /bank-accounts/{id}/history documents every balance figure as a string ("available_balance_credit": "100", "available_balance_debit": "-200") while the same concepts are integers on the bank account object and int64 in settlement reports; and the loan sale object mixes types within a single response ("amount": 100900 as an integer alongside "sold_principal_receivable": "100000" and "paid_servicing_fee": "100" as strings). Two further inconsistencies were observed live: the same created_at field name is returned at three different precisions in one webhook payload (2026-09-02T18:50:54Z, 2026-09-02T18:50:53.98882748Z, and 2026-09-02T18:50:39.719Z on the endpoint object), and transfer status is returned uppercase live (COMPLETED, HOLD, CANCELED) while the documented example response shows lowercase ("status": "completed"). These are confined to secondary/reporting endpoints and to value formatting rather than the core balance and transfer reads. [https://docs.column.com/api/bank-account/get-bank-account-summary-history; https://docs.column.com/api/lending; live observations 2026-09-02]

Partial
C2.3

Structured errors

The error body is excellent and among the best available: a stable machine-readable code, a categorical type, a developer message, a documentation_url, and a details object naming the exact offending field, with all codes and types enumerated in the docs. Verified live: {"code":"mandatory_parameter_missing","details":{"field":"entity_id"},"type":"validation_error"}. The exact limitation is HTTP status semantics, which the check requires for a yes. Column's own documentation states 404 means "Not Found. The requested resource does not exist", but not-found conditions return HTTP 400 in practice, confirmed on three separate resources (bank_account_not_found, and resource_not_found on both /transfers/ach/{id} and /counterparties/{id}). Additionally a 401 returns an entirely empty body (content-length: 0) with no structured error, despite authentication_error being a documented error type. A third observation: appending stray trailing characters to an entity ID is silently truncated and returns HTTP 200 with the prefix-matching record rather than rejecting the malformed input, so a string-concatenation bug in operator code fails silently instead of erroring. [https://docs.column.com/working-with-the-api/errors; live observations 2026-09-02]

Partial
C2.4

Duplicate prevention

Idempotency-Key is documented and enumerated across every consequential write, including all six transfer rails, account creation, entity creation, and loan disbursements, payments and sales, with a stated 255-character limit and a 30-day expiry. Verified live: two identical POST /transfers/book calls carrying the same key both returned the same book_3Img94P2ha0iEqstLpU1J5eCSlo and exactly one transfer existed afterward. [https://docs.column.com/working-with-the-api/idempotency; live 2026-09-02]

Yes
C2.5

Graceful handling under load

A 429 Too Many Requests row appears in the documented status-code table, satisfying the minimum. The exact limitation: there is no recovery guidance of any kind. A full-corpus search for rate-limit, throttling and backoff guidance returned zero matches outside the single 429 table row and an unrelated passage about webhook delivery retries. No Retry-After header and no RateLimit-* headers were returned on any of 40 consecutive live requests. The check explicitly does not penalize withholding exact limit numbers, but it does require either a machine-readable Retry-After or explicit numeric backoff guidance, and neither exists. [https://docs.column.com/working-with-the-api/errors; live header capture across 40 requests, 2026-09-02]

Partial
C2.6

Pagination for large collections

Documented cursor pagination via starting_after and ending_before with an explicit stable ordering guarantee ("return objects in inverse chronological order"), a has_more next-page signal, and a limit of 1 to 100. Verified live: a full multi-page walk of /entities at limit=1 traversed both pages correctly, with has_more: true then false; /transfers additionally returns total_results_count. [https://docs.column.com/working-with-the-api/pagination; live 2026-09-02]

Yes
C2.7

Bulk or incremental export

A dedicated bulk export path exists: POST /reporting schedules bank_account_transaction and bank_account_summary settlement reports delivered in CSV, JSON or Parquet, retrieving full datasets without per-record calls. Incremental sync is independently available on standard list endpoints via created.gt/gte/lt/lte plus cursors, verified live. [https://docs.column.com/guides/reporting; https://docs.column.com/api/reporting; live 2026-09-02]

Yes
C2.8

Webhook security and delivery reliability

All three required elements are present. Signatures: HMAC-SHA256 over the raw payload in a Column-Signature header, with a unique per-endpoint secret ("secret keys may not be shared across webhook endpoints"); a live endpoint creation returned its own whsr_ secret. Retry policy: "retry up to 25 times within 3 days with exponential backoffs. The first retry happens one minute after the original failed delivery." Replay/idempotency guidance: "An event may occasionally be delivered multiple times ... We advise you to guard against duplicated event receipts by making your event processing idempotent", plus explicit out-of-order delivery guidance. Verified live end to end: an endpoint was registered, a real book.transfer.completed event was generated by a live book transfer, and the resulting delivery attempt was recorded and readable via GET /webhook-deliveries/endpoint/{id} with the full event payload and scheduled_at. As disclosed above, signature verification is documentation-graded because the endpoint was deliberately pointed at a non-resolving domain. [https://docs.column.com/working-with-the-api/events-and-webhooks; live 2026-09-02]

Yes
C2.9

Concurrency and conflict control

Documented conflict semantics exist for state transitions: invalid_record_status is an enumerated error code, and it was returned live (with type: transfer_error and the message "You cannot perform this function on a transfer with this status") when a completed transfer was re-canceled. The exact limitation: the other half of the check is entirely absent. There is no optimistic concurrency of any kind, no ETag, no If-Match, no version field and no 409 status anywhere in the documentation or the OpenAPI spec, and no documented concurrency limits or behavior; searches for "optimistic", "lost update", "race condition", "simultaneous" and "concurren" across the full corpus returned zero matches. Concurrent PATCH calls to the same record are therefore last-write-wins with no way to detect a lost update. [full-corpus search and OpenAPI spec scan; live 2026-09-02]

Partial
C2.10

Versioning and backward compatibility

A versioning scheme exists and is pinnable: the OpenAPI spec carries info.version: "1.3" and is released under tagged versions (v1.1 on 2026-05-11, v1.2 on 2026-06-10, v1.3 on 2026-08-14), and Column states "The spec is versioned, so you can pin your integration and track changes over time." Deprecation is signaled in-band, with 8 operations carrying deprecated: true. The exact limitation: there is no explicit version identifier in the request path or a header (the server is a bare https://api.column.com/ with no version segment and no version header in the spec), and no documented backward-compatibility policy defining breaking versus non-breaking changes and no deprecation window or notice period. A corpus search for "deprecation", "sunset", "breaking", "backwards compat" and "notice period" as policy statements returned no policy. [https://docs.column.com/ "Built on OpenAPI"; https://github.com/column/openapi commit history; OpenAPI spec scan]

Partial
C2.11

Request traceability

Every response, on both success and failure, carries a unique request-id (for example apir_3ImfNVYe6wGzumSXH5aTyIoI8Gk), alongside a platform-id, confirmed unique per request across 40 consecutive live calls including the 401 path. The exact limitation: it is entirely undocumented. Searches for "request-id" and "request_id" as a response header returned no matches in the documentation corpus, and the header does not appear in the OpenAPI spec; no guidance tells an operator to quote it when contacting support@column.com. The identifier is present and usable in practice but a developer would only find it by inspecting raw headers. [live header capture 2026-09-02; documentation and spec search]

Partial
C2.12

Service availability and status transparency

A public status page monitors four components (Sandbox, Production, FedWire, FedACH), publishes 90-day uptime percentages per component, carries a dated incident history feed, and offers email, SMS, Slack and RSS subscriptions. Both an uptime signal and incident history are present, which is what the check requires; no public SLA document was found, but the check accepts uptime or SLA information. [https://status.column.com/, observed 2026-09-02]

Yes

Scoped credentials

Fine-grained resource and action scoping, well beyond role-based. Each transfer type (ACH credits, ACH debits, ACH returns, wires, deposited checks, issued checks, book transfers, realtime transfers, stop payments) is set independently, "so a key can be granted Create & Edit on ACH credits while remaining View Only on wires", and Transfers, Entities and Bank Accounts permissions can each be overridden per entity and per account. Multiple distinct keys are supported and separate keys per integration are the documented recommendation: "One key per workload. Each of your applications should use a separate key. Keys cannot manage other keys via the API by design, which is a security control rather than a barrier. [https://docs.column.com/guides/api-key-security; https://docs.column.com/guides/roles-and-permissions]

Yes

Separate credentials and fully separated data. Verified live: the supplied test_ key operated exclusively on a sandbox platform, and every write in this evaluation was contained there. Category 4 · Docs & AI-Ready

3.8 / 5
C4.1

Complete self-serve reference

The reference is public, complete in scope and example-rich: every resource has an object definition, enumerated parameters, and worked curl request plus JSON response examples across 137 paths and 177 operations. The exact limitation is accuracy, established by four separately confirmed defects rather than one blemish. First, the worked example under "Setup a webhook endpoint" posts to https://api.column.com/api/webhook_endpoints, which returns HTTP 404 live, while the correct /webhook-endpoints returns 200. Second, three rendered response examples show "status": "completed" in lower case while the API returns COMPLETED, so a developer who trusts the documented example and compares on the string writes a bug that only surfaces at runtime. Third, the documented status table promises 404 for missing resources while the API returns 400, so error handling written from the docs misclassifies. Fourth, POST /interests/configs is listed among idempotency-supported endpoints but does not exist anywhere in the OpenAPI spec. The API reference pages themselves are correct where they overlap with these flows, which is why this is partial and not no, but the accumulated drift means the documentation cannot be trusted without live verification. [https://docs.column.com/working-with-the-api/events-and-webhooks; https://docs.column.com/working-with-the-api/idempotency; llms-full.txt L1987, L10046, L10116, L10166, L1511; live re-verification 2026-09-02]

C4.4

Kept current

Currency signals exist and are partly strong: a changelog runs from 2021-07-27 through 2026-07-30 across 67 dated entries and does record genuine API changes including explicit deprecations (for example the 2022-01-05 entry listing deprecated and merged webhook events, and the 2021-07-27 entry deprecating amount[cents]), and the OpenAPI spec has dated, versioned releases as recently as three weeks before this run. The exact limitation: the signal has become thin and irregular. The versioned spec releases that would fill that gap carry commit messages of only "[v1.2] API updates" and "[v1.3] API updates" with no itemized notes, so an operator cannot see what changed between spec versions without diffing 755 KB of YAML themselves. Category 5 · Access & Cost

11.3 / 15
C5.1

Self-serve API key

Once an account exists, credential creation is fully self-serve with no sales call, support ticket or key-approval step: "You can create sandbox and production keys in the developer dashboard", and the getting-started flow is simply "navigate to the dashboard and copy your api-key". Confirmed live: the operator-supplied sandbox key was self-issued and authenticated immediately against a platform provisioned the same day. [https://docs.column.com/working-with-the-api/authentication; https://docs.column.com/guides/getting-started; live 2026-09-02]

Yes
C5.3

Not commercially gated

There are no plans or tiers at all, so API access is not locked behind a premium or top-tier plan in the conventional sense, and the sandbox is free, complete ("Every route is sandboxed") and self-serve. The exact limitation is that production access carries commercial conditions an operator can neither see nor clear unaided. Column publishes no pricing whatsoever: https://column.com/pricing/ now returns only a 50-byte meta-refresh redirect to the homepage, and no pricing link appears anywhere in the site navigation, so the cost of entry is undiscoverable from first-party materials. Production is not self-serve: Column must enable the live environment, and the going-live guide states "Depending on your intended use case, you may have to provide us with additional details about your business before you are able to go to production and process real money." ACH debit origination additionally requires funding a program reserve account whose balance must exceed rolling 60-day debit volume, with those funds locked ("Once funds are moved to this account they will be locked, and you will be unable to move them"). The property-management offering's only entry points are a "Contact us" form and propertymanagement@column.com. Per the check, KYC/KYB and compliance verification legally required to conduct real banking are excluded from this judgment and are not counted against Column; what remains, and what drives the partial, is undisclosed commercial terms, locked collateral, and the absence of any self-serve path to production. [https://column.com/pricing/ observed 2026-09-02; https://docs.column.com/guides/going-live; https://column.com/property-management/]

Partial

The bottom line for a property manager

Column is a real, nationally chartered bank with one of the best banking APIs you can build on, and unlike almost every banking API you will be pitched, there is no sponsor bank in the middle: Column holds the money itself. You could build automated owner disbursements, vendor payments, rent collection by ACH debit, check issuance and deposit, and a reconciliation feed with running balances, and hand an AI agent a key that reads balances but is structurally incapable of moving a dollar. What you cannot do is treat it as a substitute for your PMS or your trust accounting. The API has no concept of a property, unit, lease or tenant, and despite a polished property-management page promising trust reconciliation and owner disbursements, there is not one property-management endpoint in the API itself. Two words in the marketing deserve care: the trust accounts in Column's changelog are legal estate-planning trusts, not client trust accounting, and the PM-specific promises are relationship features reached through a sales conversation, not documented API capabilities. Biggest strength is control and safety; biggest limitation is operational opacity; and the biggest business limitation is not technical at all.

About this run

Column sponsors this report, and this row has been through six independent grading runs, more than any other platform here. The first three graded a frozen evidence packet blind and scored 85, 95 and 92. Column then sent a written clarification, it was added to the packet, and three further blinded runs scored 85, 86 and 86. The published 85 is the reconciled result. The counterintuitive part is worth saying plainly: more information moved graders toward the stricter mark, not away from it. The only two runs that passed Column on commercial gating were the two that had not read Column's statement, and all three that did read it marked it partial. Three reasons converged. The methodology requires a publicly auditable citation for a pass, and private correspondence is not one. The statement addresses the cost of API access but not the separate finding that several capabilities need Column to switch them on per platform. And on the reserve, the statement confirms that a funded reserve is required and disputes only how it is sized. One reading stays legitimate and is recorded rather than averaged away: a grader who takes gated to mean strictly a purchasable plan ladder would pass it, producing 93 and an A. Worth crediting Column for the outcome anyway: without their statement there was no first-party evidence either way on cost, the check would have been unverified, and the whole score would have been withheld.
