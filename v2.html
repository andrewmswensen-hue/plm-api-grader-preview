The problem.

Every platform will tell you their API is great. I wanted to find out if that's actually true.

Property managers should have access to their own data. These platforms are fantastic, but they vary wildly, both in how sophisticated the API is and in their commercial posture: how willing they are to let us reach our own data. Some of that is a technology problem. A lot of it is a business decision.

Grading that objectively wasn't possible at scale until recently. With AI, it is. The idea came from SaaStr's API Report Card for B2B SaaS. I wanted one for our industry.

The method.

One fixed grading prompt, run against every platform below. Same criteria, same process, every time.

Download it and check it yourself; every result traces back to it. If you think there's a better way to grade this, I want to hear it.

Methodology v1.1

What gets graded

Five categories, each worth a fixed share of the 100 points. A category earns the fraction of its checks it passes, times its maximum.

1

Functional Coverage and Usefulness 15 pts

Can you get the data and take the actions your business runs on, and change them, not just read them? Coverage is scored against a predetermined critical, important, and optional weighting.

2

API Design, Reliability, and Operability 10 pts

Is the API built so your code, no-code tools, and AI agents behave predictably instead of breaking, across typing, errors, idempotency, rate limits, pagination, bulk export, webhook security, concurrency, versioning, traceability, and uptime transparency?

3

Access Control and Safe Automation 5 pts

Can you safely hand a limited slice of access to an app or an AI agent, and shut it off?

4

Documentation and AI-Agent Readiness 5 pts

Can a developer, or an AI coding tool, understand the API and build against it correctly?

5

Accessibility and Cost 15 pts

Can you get access without a sales call or a forced upgrade?

How the grade is calculated

The five category scores add up to a raw total out of 50, which is doubled to a score out of 100 and mapped to a letter grade: A+ at 97 and above, down to F below 60. Scores are absolute, never curved against other platforms. A number is only published when the run clears the methodology's verification bar; otherwise the score is withheld.

Fair and transparent, on purpose.

This report is sponsored by Column, whose support makes it possible. Column is graded by the same process as everything else here, and their sponsorship did not influence their ranking.

Why I'm giving this away.

This is free because I want to push the industry forward: real pressure on vendors to build better APIs, and to stop treating basic data access as a paid upgrade.
